Zero-Knowledge Architecture & Recovery Limitations

Why True AES-256-GCM Encryption Has No Secret Backdoors

1. Zero-Knowledge Key Derivation

In SecureVault 2.0, your master password is never stored anywhere on our servers in plaintext. When you enter your master password, it is passed through the scrypt key-derivation function (KDF) with a cryptographically random salt to derive a 256-bit AES encryption key directly in memory.

2. Why There is No "Reset Password" Backdoor

Many traditional apps offer a "Forgot Password" link that emails a reset link. However, in true client/vault cryptography, if the server could decrypt your files after resetting your password, it means the server had a master backdoor key or stored your plaintext keys all along.

SecureVault 2.0 adheres strictly to genuine authenticated encryption: If you lose your master password, no administrator, engineer, or automated system can mathematically decrypt your files.

3. Recommended Safeguards & Best Practices

  • Password Manager: Store your vault master password in an offline or encrypted password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePass).
  • 2FA Emergency Codes: Download and print your 2FA backup recovery codes to ensure you are never locked out of your account login.
  • Emergency Sheet: Keep a physically secured copy of your master credentials in a fireproof safe or safety deposit box.
